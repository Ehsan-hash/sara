# Code of conduct

Sara is built by people with different backgrounds, skills and opinions. This page says how we
treat each other in this project: in issues, pull requests, discussions and anywhere else we talk
about it.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Many people here are new to coding or new to this project. Questions
  are welcome; nobody should feel silly for asking one.
- **Be respectful.** Disagree with ideas, not with people. Assume good intent, and say what you
  mean plainly.
- **Be helpful.** Give feedback that someone can act on. Credit other people's work.
- **Be honest.** Say when you're unsure, and own your mistakes.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or personal or political attacks.
- Discriminating against anyone, or demeaning anyone, for who they are: for example their age,
  body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience level, nationality,
  appearance, race, religion or sexual orientation.
- Sexual language or images aimed at a person, and unwanted attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information (such as an address or real name) without their
  permission.
- Sharing sexual content involving minors, in any form. This is reported to the authorities.
- Repeatedly pushing a point after people have asked you to stop.

Sara is a companion app and some people use her for adult conversation on their own servers. That
is their private business. This project's shared spaces stay professional: keep adult content out
of issues, pull requests, screenshots and test data.

## If something goes wrong

If someone's behaviour worries you, contact the maintainers privately: use GitHub's private
vulnerability reporting (the **Security** tab → **Report a vulnerability**) and start the title with
"Conduct", or email **sara@backstagecoding.com**. Reports are kept confidential.

The maintainers decide what to do, from a quiet word to removing comments or blocking someone from
the project, depending on what happened. Anyone asked to stop is expected to stop at once.

These rules apply to the project's spaces and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.
